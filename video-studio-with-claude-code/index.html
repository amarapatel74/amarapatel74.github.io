Build a video studio with Claude Code

Amar Patel · 29 October 2026

Paste one prompt into Claude Code, and over a day or two it builds a local, open-source stack that turns a script and a recording of your app into short videos: screen capture, a synthetic voiceover, generated music, motion and captions.

The stack explaining itself. Everything in this film, the voice included, came out of the repo the prompt below builds. 1 min 40 s, with captions.

The prompt

Copy this into Claude Code in an empty folder beside your app. Fill in the three highlighted parts first; everything else works as written.

I want short videos that show people how to use my app: tutorials and promos for YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels (9:16), plus 16:9 cuts for LinkedIn. I don't want to be on camera, and I don't have time for video production.

Think like an architect building a new stack. Make a new repo for it beside my app. Before building anything, assess the open-source options and recommend one per job, with its licence.

GOAL: script in, film out. One command turns a script file and a recording of my app into a finished MP4: screen capture, synthetic voiceover, music, motion and captions.

CONSTRAINTS
- Runs locally. My machine: [GPU and memory, e.g. "NVIDIA, 12 GB" or "AMD with ROCm, 16 GB" or "CPU only"].
- Only tools and model weights whose licences allow commercial use. Record each choice and its licence in docs/decisions.md. MusicGen weights are non-commercial, so never use them.
- Footage comes only from my app's demo mode, with made-up data: [how to start demo mode, or "there is none yet: build a seeded demo first"].
- Never clone or imitate a real person's voice. Design voices from written descriptions.
- I judge voices and music by ear. Give me short auditions to pick from; never decide for me.
- The house accent is [e.g. British English]. Test that the accent survives on real script lines, not just a sample.

ARCHITECTURE
- Four steps: capture, voice, compose, render. Each reads files and writes files, so any step can run again on its own.
- Capture is data: each video's clicks, scrolls and waits live in its script.json. One runner (Playwright, frames from Chrome's screencast at 60 fps) plays them and records when each beat lands.
- Voice: one house voice for every video. Keep approved clips and record what each one says; re-voice only lines whose words changed. Keep a pronunciation dictionary (IPA if the engine takes it). After voicing, a speech recogniser checks each clip for missing or swapped words.
- Compose: an HTML and GSAP composition rendered frame by frame (HyperFrames). Lines land on their beats. Captions take the script's words and the transcript's timings, matched line by line. Music dips under the voice, mastered to -16 LUFS.
- Motion: a small set of opt-in tools (pointer and click ripple, one slow zoom, freeze-and-label, speed ramp, transitions, motion blur), used where they help. Default to a still camera.

BUILD ORDER (stop at every * for my decision)
1. Assessment and stack choice *
2. Spike: one 20-30 s video end to end, rough is fine
3. Voice auditions: about 6 descriptions x 2 takes, each reading real script lines *
4. Motion toolkit, then music auditions in several styles; loop the pick on its bar lines *
5. Capture as data, then a list of the first ten videos *
6. Hardening: locked voice clips, per-line captions, pronunciation dictionary, voice check, a capture that waits for the app to be on screen
7. A 16:9 showcase film of the stack itself, made with the stack

DOCS: a README, an AGENTS.md of rules for agents, and docs/ for architecture, making a video, voice, sound, motion, decisions and roadmap. Commit after each phase I approve.

How it fits together

A script and two house assets go in; each step writes files the next one reads. Change a line and only Voice, Compose and Render run again; change a click and only Capture does.

The plan

Seven phases took us about a day and a half of elapsed time, most of it waiting on renders and listening. Times are what this build took, on one desktop with a 16 GB AMD GPU.

Lessons

Most of our time went on voice. Put these in the prompt, or tell Claude Code when they come up.

ProblemCauseWhat fixed it
Every voice sounded American, even when described as BritishThe designed voice drifts towards its training data, more so when copied onto new linesKokoro in British mode: it turns words into British sounds before speaking. Then blend a Kokoro voice towards the timbre you liked
One word kept coming out wrong ("Meh" read as "may")The engine guesses the sound from the spellingA pronunciation dictionary with IPA. Only your ear can judge it: the speech recogniser heard "May" for every spelling
Re-voicing undid an approved takeEach voicing is a fresh random takeKeep approved clips in git, record what each one says, re-voice only changed lines
Questions sound like statementsKokoro has no control over intonationStill open. Pitch marks and pitch lifts were too small to hear; reword, or test a more expressive engine
Captions jumped or the build failedThe recogniser heard "to-do" as two words, so counts across the whole film driftedMatch words line by line; join split words; end each line's last word at its clip's end
Viewers felt seasickThe camera pushed in on every clickA still camera with one slow zoom where text must be read; film the desktop layout
Capture timed out now and thenIt waited for a button that was hidden or absent in some layoutsWait for something visible in every layout; reload once; save a screenshot on failure
Music stopped two thirds through a long filmThe theme was a 60 s takeLoop the whole take on its bar lines
Claps and snares fought the voiceSharp hits in the speech rangeAsk for no claps; cut the music around 2.5 kHz; dip it while the voice speaks
Screen recording came out at half sizePlaywright's recorder paints CSS pixelsTake frames from Chrome's screencast and acknowledge each one first, which keeps 60 fps

What you need

A desktop with a 16 GB GPU, about 15 GB of disk for models, and a Claude Code plan. Nothing else costs money: every tool below allows commercial use.

  • Kokoro uses eSpeak (GPL) for words it doesn't know; it runs as a separate library.
  • Kokoro's GPU path failed on our AMD card, so it runs on the CPU. On NVIDIA it may run on the GPU.
  • First runs download about 9 GB of voice models and 5 GB of music models.
